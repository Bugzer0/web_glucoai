## What Is an Ambulatory Glucose Profile (AGP)?

The ambulatory glucose profile is a standardized, single-page report generated by most modern continuous glucose monitors. Think of it as a master summary of your glucose metrics over a specific timeframe, typically fourteen days. Instead of forcing you to scroll through endless daily graphs, the AGP compresses all your data into one unified visual snapshot.

According to the American Diabetes Association (ADA), the AGP was designed to create a universal language for glucose data analysis. It allows you and your healthcare team to quickly spot trends, evaluate stability, and measure your overall metabolic control without getting lost in daily fluctuations. 

When you look at an AGP report, you are not just seeing a collection of random numbers. You are seeing a composite picture of how your body responds to food, stress, sleep, and movement day after day.

## The Core Components of Your AGP Report

To successfully interpret your report, you need to know which sections deserve your primary attention. While the page may contain several data points, a few key metrics tell the most important story about your metabolic health.

### 1. Time in Range (TIR)
Time in range represents the percentage of time your blood sugar stays within your target range, usually set between 70 to 180 mg/dL. This is one of the most valuable numbers on your report. Aiming for a higher time in range means your glucose is remaining stable and avoiding dangerous highs and lows.

### 2. Glucose Variability and the CV
Glucose variability measures how much your blood sugar bounces up and down throughout the day. It is typically expressed as the coefficient of variation (CV). Ideally, you want your CV to stay below 36%. High variability means your blood sugar swings wildly, which can lead to fatigue, brain fog, and increased long-term metabolic stress.

### 3. The Median Line (The 50th Percentile)
Right in the middle of the main AGP graph, you will find a dark line known as the median line. This represents your typical glucose level at any given hour of the day over the reporting period. If this line creeps above your target range during specific hours—such as every afternoon around 3:00 PM—you have found a recurring pattern that needs attention.



## Uncovering Hidden Patterns in the 24-Hour Curve

The most striking feature of the AGP report is the 24-hour visual graph, which looks a bit like a ribbon or a wave stretched across a single day. This graph is where you find the most actionable insights about your daily habits.

The shaded areas around the median line represent different percentiles of your data, usually showing the 25th to 75th percentiles (the inner band) and the 10th to 90th percentiles (the outer band). 

### Spotting Post-Meal Spikes
Look at the times corresponding to your usual meals. Do you notice a sharp upward bulge in the shaded bands about an hour after breakfast or lunch? This visual spike tells you that your current meal composition or portion size is causing a rapid elevation in your blood sugar. 

### Identifying Overnight Trends
Examine the overnight hours, typically midnight to 6:00 AM. A flat, stable line indicates restful metabolic recovery. However, if you see your glucose steadily climbing in the early morning hours, you may be experiencing the dawn phenomenon—a natural surge of hormones that prompts the liver to release glucose before you wake up.



## Practical Steps to Analyze Your Own AGP Report

Reading your report does not have to be a passive exercise. You can take a structured, step-by-step approach to uncover your body's unique patterns.

- **Choose a consistent reporting window**: Review a 14-day or 30-day report to ensure you have enough data to spot genuine habits rather than one-off anomalies.
- **Check your time in range first**: Note what percentage of your day is spent in your target zone. Set a small, achievable goal to improve that number by 5% over the next month.
- **Scan for recurring peaks**: Identify the exact hours where your median line or shaded bands consistently exceed your target threshold.
- **Correlate with your log**: Match those peak hours with your daily habits. Did a high-stress work meeting or a high-carbohydrate lunch happen right before that spike?
- **Make one targeted adjustment**: Choose a single pattern to address—such as adding a post-meal walk after dinner—and observe how your AGP report shifts in the following weeks.
